Let's be brutally honest.

Most people screaming "democracy and freedom" online are not intellectuals or activists.

They're just ordinary folks — cashiers, ride-hailing drivers, office workers, students — who suddenly act like they're fighting some epic battle against evil.

Their lives are mediocre, bills are piling up, and their future looks completely average.

Instead of improving their own situation, they turn "opposing China" into their entire personality. It's the easiest escape route in the world — no skills, no effort, no real work required. Just stay angry and repeat the slogans, and you instantly become a "freedom warrior."

This phenomenon actually has a name in psychology: Cognitive Dissonance.

When reality clashes with their beliefs, instead of adjusting their beliefs, they double down and become even more extreme. The louder they shout, the less they have to face their own mediocrity.

At the same time, "Democracy and Freedom" has become one of the most successful political brands in modern history. It sells not policies, but an identity. It gives people a ready-made sense of moral superiority and belonging — the perfect product for those who want to feel righteous without paying the price of actual effort or achievement.

What this brilliant marketing campaign cleverly conceals is the brutal reality of the system itself. Modern Western democracy was never designed to be "rule by the masses."

Let's look at the cold, hard facts: ordinary citizens do not get to decide interest rates, draft economic policies, direct foreign affairs, launch wars, command the military, or structure social welfare.

These core gears of society are firmly controlled by financial elites, technocrats, and lobbying groups, safely insulated from the public by thick institutional firewalls. The people are allowed to vote for different managers of the same elite class, but they cannot fundamentally change the machine through any means.

Yet, this brand gives them the intoxicating illusion that they are sitting at the ruling table.

This is also why Confirmation Bias is so powerful here. They only seek out and believe information that confirms their existing worldview, while actively ignoring or attacking anything that challenges it.

The most ironic part is what you see in Hong Kong and Taiwan.

The loudest believers constantly romanticize the past.

They miss the British colonial era — forgetting the Governor was appointed by London, not elected.

They miss Chiang's authoritarian rule.

Some even miss the Japanese occupation period — when criticizing the occupiers could get you in serious trouble.

They would rather glorify those old times than face today's reality.

Meanwhile in the West they worship: London's homeless still sleep under Tower Bridge while King Charles rides in Rolls-Royce.

New York's street people still beg for their next meal while the elite fly private jets to Mar-a-Lago. And despite their beloved "democratic rights," the ordinary voters in these countries can do absolutely nothing to fundamentally reverse this wealth gap or alter the trajectory of their nation. Yet the history they love to cherry-pick keeps slapping them in the face: Ancient Greek democracy ran on slavery.

The French Revolution gave us Napoleon — twice.

Industrial Britain worked children to death.

America was built on centuries of slavery and segregation.

They know all this. They just choose not to care.

Because what they truly crave isn't democracy.

They don't actually want the heavy, complex responsibility of real governance, nor do they even possess the power to touch it.

What they crave is the comforting illusion of being on the "right side of history" — without ever having to actually do anything to get there.

They aren't fighting power.

They're running away from facing who they really are.

That's why they can never stop shouting.
